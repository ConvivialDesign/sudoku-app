Common Sudoku Mistakes Beginners Should Avoid

Sudoku becomes much easier when you follow a calm and systematic process. Avoiding a few common mistakes can improve both your accuracy and your solving speed.

1. Guessing too early

Guessing can create errors that only become visible much later in the puzzle.

Instead, place a number only when you can explain why it belongs in that square.

2. Focusing on the hardest squares first

A difficult square may have several possible numbers. Spending too much time on it can slow down your progress.

Move to an easier row, column or box. New placements elsewhere may later remove the uncertainty.

3. Checking only the row

Every square belongs to a row, a column and a 3×3 box. A number must be valid in all three areas.

Before placing a number, always check:

  • The row.
  • The column.
  • The 3×3 box.

4. Forgetting to scan again

Every correct placement changes the puzzle. A row or box that was difficult a moment ago may now contain an obvious answer.

After placing a number, scan nearby rows, columns and boxes again.

5. Using too many pencil notes

Pencil notes can be useful, but filling every empty square with every possible number can make the board harder to read.

Use notes carefully and remove candidates when they are no longer possible.

6. Rushing

Speed improves naturally as your pattern recognition develops. Trying to solve too quickly often causes avoidable mistakes.

Focus on accuracy first. Faster solving will follow with practice.

A better solving routine

  1. Scan for easy numbers.
  2. Check rows, columns and boxes together.
  3. Place only numbers supported by logic.
  4. Rescan after every placement.
  5. Move away from difficult areas when necessary.

Build better solving habits

Start an easy puzzle and focus on accuracy, elimination and rescanning.
